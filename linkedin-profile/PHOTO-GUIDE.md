# Using your own photos

You already have a photo library: the **XCLUSIVE PHOTOS** folder in the Google Drive
on `xclusivesenior@gmail.com` —
<https://drive.google.com/drive/folders/1ghLupk-hBA_za3Z8fZQ_nZpeEXiDB9H2>

That folder has 40+ images in it. This page is about which ones to use, which ones
you must not use, and how to get them onto LinkedIn.

---

## 1. Read this before you post a photo of a client

Your center bills Medicaid, which makes you a HIPAA-covered entity. A photograph of
a recognizable participant is protected health information — putting it on a public
LinkedIn page is a *marketing use*, and that needs a **signed HIPAA authorization
from that person or their legal representative** before it goes up. A verbal "sure,
go ahead" is not enough, and neither is the general consent form in the admission
packet unless it specifically covers marketing and photography.

So, in order of preference:

| Safest | Use freely |
|---|---|
| ✅ The building, entrance, signage, the van | No consent needed |
| ✅ Empty activity rooms, dining room, therapy space set up for the day | No consent needed |
| ✅ Meals as plated and served | No consent needed |
| ✅ Staff — with each staff member's OK | Ask the employee |
| ⚠️ Participants photographed from behind, hands only, or far enough away to be unrecognizable | Safer, but judgment call |
| ❌ Any recognizable participant's face | **Signed HIPAA authorization first — no exceptions** |

If you have signed photo authorizations on file for particular participants, those
photos are your strongest content by far. Warm, real photos of people enjoying the
day are what makes a family pick up the phone. Just make sure the paperwork exists
before the photo goes public, and keep a note of which photo is covered by which
authorization.

## 2. Where each kind of photo goes

| Spot | What to put there | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Profile picture** | `images/profile-picture-400x400.png` (the logo in this repo) | Cropped to a circle by LinkedIn |
| **Background / cover** | `images/banner-profile-1584x396.png` | Or a wide facility photo, if you prefer a real photo |
| **Featured section** | 3–4 of your best photos: the building, an activity, a meal | This is the visual row visitors actually look at |
| **Company Page cover** | `images/banner-company-1128x191.png` | Different size from the personal one — don't mix them up |
| **Posts** | Everything else, a few at a time | Posting 3–4 photos with a short caption beats one giant album |

**If you would rather have a real photo as your banner than the designed one:**
pick a wide, bright shot of the building or a full activity room. It must work as a
very wide, short strip — LinkedIn shows roughly the middle band of it. Keep faces
and signage near the centre, and remember the profile picture sits over the
bottom-left corner.

## 3. Your files are too big — here is the quick fix

The files in that Drive folder are PNGs between **7 MB and 35 MB**. LinkedIn caps
profile and cover images at about **8 MB**, so most of them will be rejected as-is.
Converting to JPEG fixes it and you will not see a quality difference on screen.

**Easiest — from your phone (recommended):**
1. Open the **Google Drive** app → XCLUSIVE PHOTOS.
2. Tap a photo → ⋮ → **Send a copy** / **Save to device**.
3. Open the **LinkedIn app** and upload it from your camera roll.
   The app compresses it for you, so the size limit stops being your problem.

**From a Windows PC:**
1. Download the photo from Drive.
2. Right-click it → **Open with** → **Paint**.
3. **Resize** → *Percentage* → 50% → OK.
4. **File → Save as → JPEG picture**. It will come out well under 1 MB.

**From a Mac:**
1. Download the photo, open it in **Preview**.
2. **Tools → Adjust Size** → set width to `2000` pixels.
3. **File → Export** → Format **JPEG**, Quality *High* → Save.

## 4. Cropping

Don't crop anything by hand. LinkedIn gives you a zoom-and-drag cropper on every
image upload — profile picture, cover, and Featured. Upload the full photo and move
it around in that box until it looks right.

The one thing to watch: on the **cover image**, the left third is partly covered by
your profile picture on desktop, and the far left and right edges get cut off on
phones. Keep whatever matters in the middle.
